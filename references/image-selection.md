# Image Selection and Crop Quality

Choose figures and tables by their role in the paper's argument, not by fixed keywords or a fixed quota.

## Selection

- Include a problem, task, or motivation figure when it establishes the paper's central conflict.
- Include the main method, framework, pipeline, algorithm, or objective figure when it explains the contribution better than prose alone.
- Include the main result table or benchmark figure when it is the strongest evidence for the headline claim.
- Add ablation, scaling, cost, latency, case-study, error, safety, or limitation evidence only when it changes the interpretation.
- Avoid decorative figures, redundant result tables, and screenshots that the article never explains.

Typical ranges are 3–5 evidence images for a short paper, 4–7 for a regular paper, and 7–12 for a long survey, benchmark, or system paper. Treat these as guidance; use fewer or more when the paper's evidence warrants it.

## Crop quality

Every selected screenshot must include the complete figure or table body and its original caption. Preserve legends, color keys, axes, table headers, notes, and footnotes. Prefer a slightly larger complete crop over a cleaner incomplete one.

If an automatic crop is incomplete, re-crop it from the PDF before writing the final explanation. Keep the original caption visible in the screenshot; use a shorter, specific Chinese Markdown alt text to explain why the evidence matters.

Place each image immediately after the paragraph that frames the question it answers, then explain the visible evidence and its limits. Do not treat the screenshot as self-explanatory.
